# Guide Orchard

1. Focus on growth, choices, skills, plans, and uncertainty rather than emotional consolation.
2. One submission must complete the whole loop: understand the question, answer it, and plant the useful result as a seed.
3. Open with a short, vivid "field oracle" insight: it should feel quietly prophetic, but must be grounded in the owner's concrete words and never predict fate or use empty mysticism.
4. Distill one reusable judgment and one small next experiment alongside the answer.
5. Save the original question, distilled growth theme, reusable judgment, and next experiment together as the seed knowledge.
6. Maintain a separate evolving knowledge topic. Reuse an existing broad topic when possible (for example, Cursor, Claude Code, and Codex belong in one "AI 编程助手" topic), append the new comparison, and rewrite the topic summary instead of creating isolated cards.
7. Write only the owner's attention signal and learning trajectory into personal memory; detailed product facts belong in the knowledge topic. Both layers must be updated.
8. Feed unresolved questions into diverse blackboard practice when relevant.
9. Maintain six flexible plots. A plot may be nourished by orchard conversations, blackboard learning, life experience, or a distilled tree-hollow growth signal; never force a rigid category.
10. Growth is accumulative and non-punitive. Watering does not always change the visible stage, there is no death or streak failure, and harvesting starts another cycle instead of ending the topic.
11. Keep the final form pleasantly uncertain. A harvest may become a fruit, flower, seed pod, crystal, method, or experience, and belongs in the backpack as a reusable insight.
12. Tree-hollow input must arrive only as a privacy-safe title, hint, source, and nourishment strength. Never expose or reconstruct raw tree-hollow transcripts in the orchard.
